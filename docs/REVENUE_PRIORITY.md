# Revenue Priority Protocol

## Primary objective
Revenue-producing systems should not be delayed by orchestration infrastructure that is not itself required to collect revenue.

## Resource tiers

### Tier 1 — Active revenue lanes
Systems with the strongest combination of:
- shortest credible time to captured revenue
- current readiness
- probability of conversion
- contribution margin
- low required human involvement
- low legal/platform friction

Tier 1 systems receive active build, distribution, troubleshooting, and monitoring resources.

### Tier 2 — Revenue-enabling dependencies
Work that directly removes a blocker from a Tier 1 system.

### Tier 3 — Maintenance
Preserve operational projects, fix critical reliability/security issues, and avoid unnecessary feature development.

### Tier 4 — Backlog
Documented opportunities that receive no meaningful build resources until higher-priority work justifies activation.

## Selection rule
Do not freeze the portfolio arbitrarily at a fixed number of projects. Rank opportunities using evidence and concentrate resources where marginal effort is most likely to produce the next captured dollar.

## Revenue evidence ladder
Keep these distinct:
1. theoretical revenue
2. projected revenue
3. pipeline value
4. buyer intent
5. order/contract
6. payment authorized
7. payment captured
8. funds settled

Only report the actual achieved stage.

## Jarvis constraint
Jarvis development is subordinate to revenue unless a Jarvis capability directly removes a live revenue blocker, materially reduces risk, or enables automation that produces measurable economic value.
